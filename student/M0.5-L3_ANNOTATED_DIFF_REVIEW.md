## Material risk identified by Jeremy

Jeremy identified that the builder's passing percentage-variance example does not prove the requested calculation was implemented. The displayed `28.6%` uses actual rather than budget as the denominator, so the output can look plausible while implementing a different business rule.

## Deterministic evidence proposed and refined

Jeremy proposed a row with `$250,000` budget and `$275,000` actual. The independently expected results are:

- dollar variance: `$25,000`;
- percentage variance: `10%`, because `$25,000 / $250,000 = 10%`; and
- investigate: `true`, because both approved thresholds are exactly met.

This is a valid exact boundary case and directly checks the percentage calculation if the test asserts `10%`. The instructor supplied one diagnostic limitation: checking only the final investigation flag would not isolate the denominator or threshold-combination defects because the proposed implementation could still flag the row when the dollar boundary alone is met.

Additional evidence needed before acceptance includes a case where exactly one threshold is met, a zero-budget case, and an input with more than ten rows followed by a complete row-count reconciliation. These additional cases were explained by the instructor rather than claimed as independent learner evidence.

## Targeted inspection request

Jeremy was initially unsure what targeted inspection meant. The instructor explained that it means asking AI to expose and translate only the small implementation areas controlling:

- which value is used as the percentage denominator;
- whether thresholds are joined by `and` or `or`;
- what happens when budget is zero; and
- whether the final return preserves every row.

Jeremy was not asked to restate that supplied explanation. A genuinely different transfer will assess whether he can apply it.
