# M0.5-L5 — Explaining Human and AI Contributions Without Inflation

Lesson ID: `M0.5-L5`

Checkpoint: `M0.5-C5`

Skill ID: `SK-AI-BUILDER-CONTRIBUTION-001`

Status: Prepared for authorized teaching; learner checkpoint not yet attempted

Estimated required learner time: 30–45 minutes

Primary capability: describing AI-assisted work accurately by separating business ownership, technical direction, AI execution, human verification, assistance, and limitations

Applicable resume-level gate: none; this completes foundational builder-supervision evidence but does not itself earn a technical resume label

Project contribution: produces the contribution statement used to describe later course projects without claiming unaided implementation or professional experience

Prerequisite evidence: `evidence/LEARNER_CHECKPOINT_M0.5-C4.md`

## Scenario and user

Jeremy reviews the actual evidence from Module 0.5 and creates a concise, defensible account of what he decided, directed, inspected, verified, and learned versus what AI generated or executed. He then evaluates a genuinely different project description that overstates or understates the human contribution.

## Why this matters

AI-assisted builder work is valuable only when the contribution claim remains accurate. “I built it” can conceal substantial AI execution; “AI did everything” can erase real human ownership of requirements, risks, verification, and release decisions. A defensible statement names both sides and does not turn synthetic course work into professional experience.

## Learning objectives

By the checkpoint, Jeremy will be able to:

1. distinguish outcome and business-rule ownership from implementation execution;
2. identify what he personally diagnosed, constrained, inspected, and verified;
3. identify what AI generated, modified, or ran;
4. disclose the highest substantive assistance used;
5. state evidence and limitations without inflating independence; and
6. correct an inaccurate contribution claim in a genuinely different scenario.

## Teaching method

### Teach

Briefly teach a five-part contribution model: outcome ownership, human decisions, AI execution, verification evidence, and limitations/assistance. Model it on a short adjacent example.

### Practice

Use the completed M0.5 artifacts and evidence. Jeremy identifies his own substantive contributions and the AI-executed work. AI may organize Jeremy's judgments into a concise contribution statement, but Jeremy must approve its accuracy.

### Demonstrate

Give Jeremy a different synthetic project description containing at least one inflated and one understated claim. With reduced scaffolding, he identifies the inaccuracies and rewrites the description within the evidence boundary.

## Learner deliverable

A concise contribution record containing:

1. the project or course-work context;
2. what Jeremy personally specified, decided, diagnosed, modified or directed, inspected, and verified;
3. what AI generated, edited, or executed;
4. deterministic evidence supporting the claim;
5. the maximum assistance used;
6. known limitations and unearned capabilities; and
7. one short external-facing statement that clearly labels the work as a synthetic, AI-assisted course project.

## Verification boundary

- Trace every material claim to completed evidence from M0.5.
- Do not convert AI execution into an unaided coding claim.
- Do not erase Jeremy's genuine ownership of requirements, risk, scope, diagnosis, and verification.
- Distinguish completed synthetic course work from professional experience.
- Preserve every unearned resume-level gate.
